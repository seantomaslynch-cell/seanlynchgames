Gridiron GM game day, depth chart and player development

Game day in Gridiron GM is decided long before kickoff. The depth chart, your schemes, your coaches and how hard you practice all feed the ratings the simulation uses, and the offseason turns snaps into growth. This guide covers those rules with the actual numbers. For the salary cap and the draft, see the other guides.

The depth chart

  • The game plays on unit ratings built from who is first on your depth chart, healthy, fresh and in form. Moving a corner from third to first changes your pass defense.
  • Starters are one quarterback, one running back, three receivers, one tight end, two tackles, three interior linemen, two edge rushers, two defensive tackles, three linebackers, three cornerbacks, two safeties, a kicker and a punter.
  • A player's live rating is not his overall. Fatigue takes up to about 7.5 points at 100, form adds or subtracts up to 10, morale nudges it, and a lingering injury costs 3.
  • Never leave a slot empty. An empty quarterback slot counts as a 25, far worse than any backup.
  • Kick and punt returners have their own chart, two deep each. If the man in the slot is hurt, the best healthy candidate returns.

Schemes and scheme fit

  • Offensive schemes are Air Raid, West Coast, Spread Option, Power Run, Wide Zone and Pro Style. Defensive schemes are 4-3 Base, 3-4 Base, Nickel Heavy, Pressure and Two-High Shell.
  • Power Run has a run factor of 1.20 and a pass factor of 0.90. Air Raid is the reverse at 1.16 passing and 0.86 running. On defense, Pressure raises the rush to 1.22 and cuts coverage to 0.86, while Two-High Shell does the opposite at 1.18 coverage and 0.90 rush.
  • A player is graded on half the gap between the ratings his scheme wants and his overall, capped at 7 points either way. The roster labels it Ideal fit (+4 or more), Good fit (+2), Awkward fit (-2) and Poor fit (-4 or worse).
  • Your coordinators set the schemes, with the head coach's scheme as a fallback.

Your weekly gameplan

  • Gameplan sliders run from 0 to 100 and start at 50, except pass ratio, which runs from 30% to 75% and starts at 56%. The others are aggression (fourth downs and deep shots), blitz, tempo, red zone, third and long, and late lead. Fast tempo means more plays and more fatigue.
  • Once a week you can add one weekly call: Run at their front, Take shots downfield, Max protect or Send pressure. The Office shows the opponent's rank in the matching unit. Each call has a fixed cost, so it helps a little against a weak unit and hurts a little against a strong one. The effect is roughly a point of margin, not a win. It applies to that regular-season game only.
  • In Coach Mode you call plays yourself, and each call is worth the same as your staff's on average.

Coaching staff

  • You hire a head coach, an offensive coordinator and a defensive coordinator. Each has seven skills: Offensive Mind, Defensive Mind, Player Development, Game Management, Motivator, Discipline and Talent Eye.
  • Most staff effects are small, roughly a two-point spread for a great staff. Development is the big one: it blends the head coach at 40% and each coordinator at 30%.
  • Four position coaches (quarterbacks, skill positions, the line, and the back seven) each develop only their own group. Their multiplier runs from about 0.88 to 1.14, and an empty chair sits at 0.85, below the worst hire.
  • Coordinators on a strong unit can get head coaching jobs elsewhere. If yours is hired away, you fill the vacancy before camp, and the new hire brings his own scheme.

Practice, rest and wear

  • Each week you set practice intensity for offense, defense and special teams. Full Pads gives 1.55 times the form and 1.6 times the development, adds 9 fatigue and carries 2.1 times the practice injury risk. Walkthrough cuts fatigue by 7 and injury risk to 0.4 times, but earns only 35% of the form and development.
  • Each player can take a Rest, Normal or Push plan. Rest cuts wear by 22 and injury risk to 0.55 times. Push adds 9 wear and 1.35 times the injury risk, for extra sharpness.
  • Wear grows with snaps and drains by 7 a week. Above 62 it multiplies injury risk, up to 1.85 times at 100. Running backs wear 30% faster, quarterbacks 25% slower. Computer teams rest anyone above 76.

Injuries and injured reserve

  • Knock (40% of injuries) costs nothing. Minor strain (27%) costs 1 to 2 weeks, Sprain (18%) 3 to 5, Tear (11%) 6 to 11 and Season-ending (4%) 14 to 22.
  • Injured reserve takes a player with at least 4 weeks left on his injury. He must miss at least 4 of your games, and byes do not count. To return he needs a designation to return, and you get 8 a season. Without one he stays down for the year. A full 53-man roster blocks activation.
  • A season-ending injury makes retirement 1.5 times as likely. The Iron Man trait cuts injury risk by 15%.

How players develop

  • Every offseason the league develops at once. Players grow toward a ceiling, so growth slows as they close in on it.
  • Snaps matter. For everyone except tackles and interior linemen, the growth factor runs from 0.45 with no snaps up to 0.85 at about 1,000 snaps. Linemen sit at 0.45. Experience from snaps, starts, wins and practice adds up to a further 33%.
  • Each player has a hidden development trait: Normal, Quick, Star or Superstar, with growth multipliers of 1.00, 1.35, 1.75 and 2.30. A young player who jumps 4.5 or more points may reveal a better trait.
  • Position decides the cliff. Running backs peak at 25 and decline at 1.60. Cornerbacks peak at 26 at 1.42. Quarterbacks peak at 28 at 0.80, and kickers and punters at 31 at roughly 0.45.
  • The Training Complex scales growth by 9% per level above the first. The Development slider moves your own players' growth from 1.12 on Easy to 0.86 on Hardest.
  • The progression report shows each of your players' ratings before and after, with snaps. It compares young starters (500 or more snaps) against young players who sat (under 100), so you can see whether starting the rookie paid off.

Frequently asked questions

Why did my young player not improve this offseason?

Growth depends on snaps. A non-lineman's growth factor runs from 0.45 with no snaps up to 0.85 with a full season of them, so a starter grows about twice as fast as a player who sat. Development traits, your coaches, the Training Complex and age also matter, and growth slows as a player nears his ceiling.

When should I put a player on injured reserve?

Only when he is out four or more weeks, because IR is a four-game commitment. To bring him back you must spend one of your 8 designations to return per season, then he must have missed the four games and be healthy.

Why does my running back decline so fast?

Each position has its own aging curve. Running backs peak at 25 and decline fastest, while quarterbacks peak at 28 and kickers and punters at 31. Running backs also pile up wear 30% faster than most players.

Does the head coach or the position coach matter more for development?

Both count. The offseason development bonus blends the head coach (40%) and the two coordinators (30% each), and each position coach adds a multiplier of roughly 0.88 to 1.14 for his own group only. An empty position chair is worse than hiring the weakest coach available.
