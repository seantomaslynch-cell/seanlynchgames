LifeLine careers, school and money: a practical guide

In LifeLine, money is the result of choices you make years earlier. School decides which doors open, your job performance decides how fast you climb, and debt can end a good run. This guide covers the rules behind all three, so you can plan a life instead of reacting to one. Pay and prices below are the US figures, and they scale by country.

School and college

  • In school you can study harder or slack off. Each moves your school performance up or down by a few points, with a small effect on smarts and happiness.
  • Join a club. The list is School Sports Club, Debate Club, Drama Club, Chess Club, Marching Band and Volunteering.
  • You can drop out from age 16. From 18 you can sit the GED, which counts as a high school performance of 62. Your odds start at 30% plus your smarts, and they improve with each retry.
  • College is four years. You apply from 18 and need a diploma or a GED, plus a high school performance of 61 or more. If you are turned down within 20 points, you can watch an ad for one reapply.
  • Tuition is 92,000 in total at US prices. Pay cash or take a student loan at 4% interest. Loans are only offered up to age 49.
  • Pick a major from Business, Engineering, Computer Science, Law, Medicine, Fine Arts or Psychology. A matching major lowers a job's smarts requirement by 5 and starts your pay 10% higher. A mismatch costs nothing, and no job is locked behind a major.
  • Grad school needs a bachelor's degree and age 20 or older. Law School is 3 years and 145,000, with a smarts bar of 62. Medical School is 4 years and 240,000, with a bar of 72. An MBA is 2 years and 120,000, with a bar of 55. Quitting grad school costs 5 reputation.

Landing a job

  • Every job lists minimum stats, such as smarts, health, looks or reputation. Some also need a degree. The Careers tab folds the jobs you cannot take yet into one row that tells you how many there are.
  • Teens can work part time from 14 to 17. Examples are Babysitter, Paper Route, Dog Walker, Tutor and Pool Lifeguard. Part-time jobs end at 18.
  • Listed pay runs from Barista at 24,000 and Retail Clerk at 26,000, through Auto Mechanic at 44,000 and Registered Nurse at 68,000, up to Software Engineer at 98,000, Attorney at 125,000 and Physician at 165,000.
  • Jobs that pay 40,000 or more, and credentialed jobs, ask for an interview of 2 to 3 questions. Each answer plays to one of your stats, and the odds stay between 20% and 95%. Entry work, training jobs and Plus roles hire without one.
  • A failed interview closes that job for the year, so pick your applications. Once you are turned down, you get no more interviews that year.
  • Once a year, one job can open on a referral if every stat it asks for is short by no more than 10. A referral skips the interview. It never opens a Plus job, a missing degree or license, or the late first-timer wall.
  • Age matters. From 55, first-time white-collar hiring dries up. Hiring stops at 74, and a new hire at 65 or older works reduced hours at half pay.

Performance, raises and promotions

  • Every full-time job has a performance score from 0 to 100. Each year it drifts toward a target set by your smarts, health and happiness.
  • Once a year you can work harder or slack off. Either one moves performance by 12 points. Working harder costs 3 happiness and 1 health. Slacking off gives 4 happiness.
  • Merit raises stop below a performance of 50 and reach their full ceiling at 90 or more. The ceiling is a small yearly figure by tier, from 0.5% for entry jobs to 1.2% for law and medicine.
  • Promotion needs a performance of 65 or more and at least 3 years since the last one. The odds are about 10% at 70 and 20% at 80, and they top out at 30%. Pass over a year and you can watch an ad for a second look. It is free with Plus.
  • Each promotion adds an 8% base raise and a new title, from Senior to Lead, Principal and Chief. Chief is the top rung. Seniority also adds 1% a year for up to 15 years, and pay is capped at 1.3 times the listed salary.
  • Ask for a raise once a year, from a full-time job. A win is worth 2% to 5% and gives 4 happiness and 1 reputation. A no costs 3 happiness. Part-time jobs cannot ask.
  • Do not ask too often. A raise you win blocks asking again for 3 years, and each earlier win in the same job multiplies your next odds by 0.75. Asking the year after a no costs 20 points of odds.
  • Watch for a performance warning. Below 25 you are put on a plan and lose 3 happiness. Stay under 25 within 2 years and there is a 60% chance of being fired. Back at 40 or more clears the warning.
  • Firing can also come from layoffs. A bad reputation, habits or poor health raise the odds, and seniority protects you a little. After a firing you can watch an ad to negotiate on the way out, which pays 3 months of salary.

Trades, licenses and Prestige careers

  • Some trades need training first. Electrician needs 3 years as an Electrician Apprentice at 37,000. Plumber needs 3 years as a Plumbing Apprentice at 35,000. Dental Hygienist needs 2 years as a Dental Assistant at 34,000. Training pays about 60% of the trade.
  • Law is a long road. After 3 years of Law School you sit the bar exam. The odds follow your smarts, stay between 20% and 97%, and rise with each retake. If you fail, you can retake it next year. Attorney then lists at 125,000.
  • Medicine is longer. Medical School is 4 years, then a 3 year residency that pays 62,000 a year for long hours. After that you are a licensed physician at 165,000.
  • Some jobs want experience in a related one. Chef needs 3 years as a Line Cook, and News Anchor needs 4 years as a Journalist.
  • There are 11 Prestige careers, and they open with Plus. They list at the same pay as the best free job behind the same door, so their value is events and titles, not wages.

Money, debt and houses

  • Pick a spending level on the Assets tab. Frugal costs you 1 happiness, normal costs nothing, and lavish gives 2 happiness but spends the most.
  • From age 20, a warning card appears if you have no income and less than 3 years of living costs in cash. If you live at home with a living parent and have no spouse, job or home, your parents cover the rent until 25.
  • Student loans grow 4% a year and are paid off automatically from your income. A line tells you when they are paid in full.
  • A negative balance grows 9% a year. Below minus 40,000, creditors start seizing assets. Past minus 100,000 you go bankrupt. That wipes your portfolio and loans and costs 18 reputation and 12 happiness.
  • A mortgage needs a full-time salary and 20% down in cash. The rate is 5.5% over 25 years. The bank lends up to 5 times your salary across all mortgages combined, and not on bare land.
  • Miss one mortgage year and you get a warning. Miss a second year in a row and the house is foreclosed.
  • You can open a brokerage from 18. The choices are Govt Bonds, S&P Index Fund, Blue-Chip Stock, Tech Growth Stock, Gold and Crypto. Crypto is the most volatile. Capital gains are taxed at 15%, scaled by country.
  • Retirement age is 65. After that, pay tapers by 5% a year down to a floor of 35%.

Business, startups and politics

  • You can run one business at a time from age 18. There are 10 types, from a Food Truck at 30,000 and a Phone Repair Shop at 35,000 to a Cafe at 60,000 and a Gym at 150,000.
  • Pick a price tier of Budget, Standard or Premium. You can invest in quality, run marketing, expand, hire and fire, take a loan, draw profits as taxed income, or sell.
  • To franchise you need 5 locations, a reputation of 70, a quality of 60 and 8 years in business. The fee is 250,000 at US prices.
  • A startup is a separate path. You put in 15,000, 60,000 or 200,000 of your own money. You can raise funding, hire, pivot, pay dividends or sell. An IPO needs a valuation of 250,000,000 or more and a profit.
  • Politics starts at 21 and you climb one rung at a time: School Board, City Council, Mayor, Governor, Senator, President. Campaigns cost from 5,000 up to 25,000,000, and you need at least 40% of the cost in funds.
  • If you lose a race for the next rung, you keep the seat you hold. A president can serve two terms.

Questions people ask

Why will my boss not give me a raise?

Raise odds sit between 5% and 75%. They rise with your performance, your reputation and how well you get on with your boss. A raise you win blocks asking again for 3 years, and each earlier win in the same job makes the next one less likely. Asking the year after a no costs 20 points of odds.

Do I need college to earn well in LifeLine?

No. Trades such as Electrician and Plumber need training, not a degree. A degree opens the jobs that ask for one, and law and medicine also need a bar exam or a residency. A major never locks you out of a job.

What happens if I run out of money?

A negative balance grows by 9% a year. Below minus 40,000 creditors start seizing assets. Past minus 100,000 you go bankrupt, which wipes your portfolio and loans and costs reputation and happiness.

Do I have to pay to unlock careers?

No. Most careers are free. The 11 Prestige careers open with Plus, which is one optional payment with no subscription.
