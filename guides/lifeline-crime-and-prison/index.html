LifeLine crime, prison and parole guide

Crime in LifeLine has real consequences. A bad year can end in a fine, a case file that follows you for decades, or a cell where every choice costs a year of your life. This guide covers the free petty crimes, the paid Underworld pack, and everything that happens once the door closes, using the numbers in the game.

Free crime: shoplifting and pickpocketing

  • Every player can shoplift from age 14 and pick pockets from age 16. Each can be tried once a year, and each is a single roll.
  • Shoplifting gets caught 30% of the time and pays 30 to 220 when it works. Pickpocketing gets caught 35% of the time and pays 15 to 380.
  • Both cost 1 karma point just for trying, even when you get away with it.
  • A minor who is caught loses 7 reputation and 5 happiness and gets a call home. There is no fine and no cell for a child.
  • An adult who is caught has a 50% chance of a fine from 200 to 900 (never more than the cash you have), which costs 6 reputation and 4 happiness. Otherwise it is an arrest, with 8 reputation and 6 happiness gone. Shoplifting then means 1 year inside. Pickpocketing means 1 to 2 years.
  • If you own the Underworld pack, or have it on a trial, these two free buttons step aside. The pack has its own fuller versions, so you never get both.

The Underworld pack

  • Underworld is a paid content pack. It covers burglary, car theft, a shoplifting spree, arson and murder, plus a case file, detectives, heat and cold cases. It starts at age 18 and allows 2 crimes a year.
  • Each crime shows two numbers: the chance it works, and the chance you are arrested on the spot if it does not. A pickpocketing job works 72% of the time. A car theft works 58% and a shoplifting spree 64%.
  • Burglary lets you pick the house. An apartment works 70% of the time and pays 1,500 to 5,000. A suburban house works 55% and pays 6,000 to 16,000. A mansion works only 36% of the time, pays 18,000 to 45,000, and gets you caught on a miss 50% of the time.
  • With a car, you choose what happens next. Keeping it is worth 20% of its value. Selling pays 25%. Chopping it pays 15% but leaves less evidence behind.
  • Arson has two targets. Burning your own empty property for the insurance works 50% of the time and pays 55% of its value, but insurance companies have investigators. Burning an empty building of someone you hate works 60% of the time and pays nothing.
  • Murder is the darkest option. It has strict limits on who can be a target, works 50% of the time, and carries 15 to 35 years if it ends in a conviction. The game handles it in a sentence and does not describe it.
  • Practice helps a little. Each earlier success at burglary, pickpocketing, car theft or a spree adds 1 point to the odds, up to 8.

Heat, evidence and the case file

  • Heat is the street's memory of you, from 0 to 100. Every point of heat takes 0.3 points off a crime's success chance and adds 0.3 points to the chance of arrest on a miss. It fades by 5 to 9 every year.
  • Almost every crime that is not solved on the spot opens a case. Each case tracks evidence, suspicion, whether anyone saw you and whether a detective took it. A miss that you escape leaves a case with 40% more evidence than a clean job.
  • The yearly arrest chance on an open case is evidence plus suspicion, divided by 400. A witness adds 6 points, every 10 points of heat adds 1, and 8 points come off. The result never goes above 45%. The rap sheet shows this number, and it is the one the game rolls.
  • Each year, evidence fades by an amount set by the crime. A witness forgets 20% of the time. A detective adds 3 to 8 suspicion while evidence is still 25 or higher, and suspicion drops by 4 otherwise.
  • When evidence falls under 15 and nobody saw you, the case goes cold or closes for good. Serious crimes are likelier to go cold than close, and a cold case can come back 5 to 20 years later.
  • Two tools help. Laying low costs 3,000, takes heat down by 20 and cuts suspicion by 5 on open cases. You need cash for it, some heat or an open case, and a year with no crime. Ditching evidence costs 2,500, removes 15 evidence from one case (25 if it was a stolen car, which is lost), and adds 2 heat. Each can be used once a year.
  • While you are in prison, no case moves and no knock comes at the door.

When a cold case knocks

  • A detective arrives with a folder and asks about the crime. You get three answers.
  • Say nothing: the chance of a charge is 20%, plus evidence divided by 200, plus 10 points if there was a witness. It stays between 15% and 60%.
  • Lawyer up for 15,000: the same chance, cut in half.
  • Confess: you are charged for certain, but the sentence is halved. It also gives 6 happiness.
  • If the knock never comes, the case closes quietly.

Arrest, trials and sentences

  • Every arrest goes through the same path. A normal arrest sends you to a cell. You lose your job, and a dealing career ends.
  • Sentences stack. If you are handed more time while you are inside, it is added to the years you already have.
  • The paid Courtroom pack changes charges of 2 years or more. Instead of a cell, you get a trial where you can pick counsel, take a plea or face a jury, and appeal. One-year charges still go straight to a cell.
  • A sentence of 2 years or more also offers a second chance screen, because a long stretch can wreck a life.

Serving time: your choices each year

While you are inside, the prison screen replaces the normal game. Time passes with the year choices below. Each one uses up a year of the sentence, but they pay out differently. The warden's favor starts at 30 and feeds the early release roll, covered further down.

  • Serve the year: 4 favor, 4 happiness lost, 2 health lost.
  • Library: 3 to 8 smarts, 1 happiness and 5 favor.
  • Yard weights: 5 to 15 respect, 2 to 6 health, 2 looks and 2 favor.
  • Work duty in the kitchen: 8 to 12 favor, with 1 health and 1 happiness lost. It is the best route to early release.
  • Release itself costs 6 reputation and gives 6 happiness.

Riots, escapes and write-ups

  • A riot or a failed escape is a write-up. Each one costs 10 favor, and the count goes into your appeal odds.
  • Leading a riot is a minigame. Win it and you gain 28 respect, 5 reputation and 2 happiness. Lose and you add 1 to 3 years, lose 10 to 28 health and 6 happiness, and you can die.
  • An escape is also a minigame. A clean run puts you outside as a free but hunted person, with 10 happiness and 6 reputation gained. A caught escape adds 2 to 5 years, costs 5 to 20 health and 8 happiness, and counts as a write-up.
  • The classic escape button, without the minigame, works 12% of the time plus your respect divided by 400 plus your smarts divided by 500.
  • After a failed escape, a lawyer offer takes back exactly the added years and nothing else. It appears once per prison stretch, and only in the failure year and the year after. It is a rewarded offer, so it involves watching an ad.

Getting out early

  • Good time: the warden can sign off a year if your favor is 70 or higher and you have 2 or more years left. The yearly chance is your favor minus 60, divided by 200, capped at 20%. It can happen at most twice per sentence. At favor 70 it is a 5% chance. At favor 100 it is 20%.
  • The appeal: right after you are sentenced, and for one year after, you are offered one appeal. It starts at 35% and drops 5 points for every write-up, never below 15%. A win cuts a third of the years left, at least 1. It needs 2 or more years remaining and is not offered on a Courtroom pack verdict, which has its own appeal. It is an optional rewarded offer.
  • The parole year: once per sentence, an optional rewarded offer takes one year off with no roll.
  • Serving the full term is always an option too.

The yard: cellmates, crews and the warden

  • Every new sentence puts a cellmate in the other bunk. About 12% of the time each year, they move on. You can befriend them for 8 to 15 bond, with friendship at 70. You can ignore them, or fight them. A fight is a write-up, costs 8 favor, and has a 15% chance of adding a year.
  • Three crews exist. The Night Aces need no respect, The Dry Creek Vipers need 25, and The Iron Wolves need 45. A crew that comes to you, which happens about 30% of years, takes you at any respect.
  • Ranks run Prospect, Soldier, Lieutenant and Shot Caller, at standing 0, 30, 60 and 90. Tasks give 10 to 16 standing. Smuggling works 55% of the time, plus smarts divided by 400. Intimidating works 50% of the time, plus health divided by 400 and respect divided by 500. Each rank adds 4 points. Odds stay between 25% and 85%.
  • Leaving a crew costs 5 to 15 health and raises pressure. Refusing an offer raises pressure by 25.
  • The yard is dangerous. The yearly chance of being jumped starts at 8%, rises with pressure and falls with respect. A crew lowers it by 5 points plus 2 for each rank, a friend as cellmate lowers it by 3 and a rival raises it by 5. It stays between 2% and 60%, and a beating costs 5 to 15 health.
  • Talking to the warden is a trap. It gives 25 favor, but costs 15 respect and adds 30 pressure. For 3 years your risk of being attacked goes up by 15 points. Any crew drops you, and no crew takes you for the rest of the sentence.

Free or paid?

  • Free: shoplifting, pickpocketing, arrests, prison, the yard, escapes and good time.
  • Paid: the Underworld pack for the full crime menu and case file, and the Courtroom pack for trials, pleas and appeals.

Questions people ask

Can I get out of prison early in LifeLine?

Yes, in a few ways. Keep the warden's favor at 70 or higher and the warden can sign off up to 2 years of good time. A parole year can come off once per sentence, and one appeal is offered in the year you are sentenced and the year after, with a 35% base chance. Escape is possible but a failed try adds 2 to 5 years.

Which crimes are free and which need the Underworld pack?

Shoplifting (from age 14) and pickpocketing (from age 16) are free, once a year each. Burglary, car theft, a shoplifting spree, arson and murder are in the paid Underworld pack, along with the case file, heat, lay low and cold cases.

What is heat in LifeLine?

Heat is the street's memory of you, from 0 to 100. Every point lowers a crime's success chance by 0.3 points and raises the chance of arrest on a miss by 0.3 points. It fades by 5 to 9 a year, and laying low for 3,000 cuts it by 20.

How do I join a prison gang?

Three crews exist: The Night Aces need no respect, The Dry Creek Vipers need 25 and The Iron Wolves need 45. A crew that comes to you takes you at any respect. You cannot join if you have talked to the warden this sentence.
