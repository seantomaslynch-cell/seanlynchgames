Gridiron GM salary cap, contracts and draft guide

Gridiron GM is a free football general manager sim for iPhone and Android. Money decides most seasons. This guide covers how the salary cap, contracts, free agency and the draft work, so you can build a roster that stays good.

How the cap works

  • The cap starts at $255M in the first season and grows about 6.2% a year. A labor deal can change that growth rate.
  • Because the cap rises, a contract signed early gets cheaper in real terms. Sign good players before the next jump.
  • A roster holds 46 to 53 players in season, up to 90 in the offseason, plus 16 on the practice squad.
  • The minimum salary is $1.1M. Each practice squad player counts a flat $220K against the cap.
  • The offseason runs in stages: Season Review, Coaching Staff, Retirements, Tags and Re-Signings, Cap Compliance, the Negotiating Window, Free Agency, the Draft, Undrafted Signings, Minicamp and OTAs, then Training Camp. Cap Compliance is the stage where you get under the number.

Signing bonuses and dead money

  • A signing bonus is paid in cash up front but spread across the cap. The spread is capped at five years.
  • Cut a player and every unpaid bonus dollar lands on this year's cap, plus any base salary that was guaranteed. That is dead money.
  • A post-June 1 cut charges this year's share only and pushes the rest onto next year. You get two of these per league year.
  • Check the cut cost before you press the button. A star on a long deal with a big bonus is the most expensive player to cut.

Restructures and void years

  • A restructure turns base salary into bonus. You gain room now and owe the money later, spread over up to five years.
  • You can add up to three void years to stretch the bonus further. The unpaid part lands on your books as dead money when the deal runs out.
  • A restructure needs at least two years left on the deal, or one year if you add void years. Void years are not allowed on tags or rookie deals, and rookie deals cannot be reworked early at all.
  • Treat a restructure as a loan. Use it to keep a contender together, not to fix a team that is already declining.

Tags, extensions and re-signing

  • The franchise tag is a one-year deal. It pays the average of the top five cap hits at the position, or 120% of his old deal, whichever is higher. The transition tag uses the top ten and 105%.
  • Tagging a running back is cheap. Tagging a quarterback eats a large share of your room.
  • The re-sign screen shows his asking price next to the market rate, so you can see the discount for keeping him.
  • Rookie deals cannot be extended or reworked until after the third season. Plan for that wall.

Free agency and offers

  • Before the market opens there is a negotiating window. You can agree terms in principle with up to three players. Nothing is signed until the market opens, and a rival can still outbid you.
  • Free agency runs five days. The big names go on day one at a premium. Bargains show up on day four when rivals run low on money.
  • A player's price cools each day he stays unsigned. Waiting can save money, but it can also cost you the player.
  • He weighs money, contract length, the guaranteed share, how well your team wins, his role and his loyalty to you. A rejected offer tells you which of those hurt most.
  • No player signs past his maximum length at any price. Incentives count toward what he thinks he is being offered, which helps close a small gap.
  • Personalities matter. A One-Team Man values loyalty. A Spotlight player wants a starting role and will not sign to sit behind someone.

Scouting the draft

  • The draft has seven rounds and 340 prospects, so many go undrafted and move to the Undrafted Signings stage.
  • Each prospect is a range. Scouting moves him through five levels: Unknown, Rumours, Film Study, Full Workup and Locked In. Each level narrows the range.
  • You get 10 scouting hours a week, and a better scouting facility adds more. Assign them to one player, a position, a region, your watch list or the top of the board.
  • The combine tells you something about everyone, and a little more about the top 64 prospects.
  • The watch list lets you scout next year's class early, so the knowledge carries over when that class arrives.
  • Each class is deep at two positions and thin at two others. Check which before you decide where to spend picks.

Draft day and rookie contracts

  • Use the mock draft to see who is likely to be gone before your pick. It uses public grades only and changes nothing in the real draft.
  • Picks can sit in a trade with players, and future picks have a value too.
  • Rookie deals follow a slotted scale. A top pick is a real cap commitment, while late first-rounders are the best value on the board.
  • Rookie deals run four years. First-round picks have all four years guaranteed, picks 33 to 96 have two, and later picks have one.
  • First-round picks come with a fifth-year option. You choose it three seasons in. It costs a notch under the transition tag, or the full transition number for a player who has made All-Pro or won MVP. Once you exercise it, the extra year is fully guaranteed.

Frequently asked questions

How big is the salary cap in Gridiron GM?

It starts at $255M in the first season and grows about 6.2% a year, unless a labor deal changes the rate.

What does it cost to cut a player with a signing bonus?

Every unpaid bonus dollar hits this year's cap, plus any guaranteed base salary. A post-June 1 cut splits that over two years, and you get two per league year.

How does the fifth-year option work?

It applies to first-round picks. You choose it three seasons into the rookie deal. It is priced off the transition tag and is fully guaranteed once exercised.

Can I see a prospect's real rating before the draft?

Only after scouting. Each prospect is a range, and spending scouting time moves him through five levels. The top level, Locked In, narrows the range the most.
