Hardwood GM draft and trade guide: scouting, picks, deals

Hardwood GM is a free, text-based basketball general manager sim. The draft and the trade market are where most careers are won or lost. This guide covers how scouting really works, how the lottery draws, how the AI values a deal and which salary rules block a trade.

Scouting is fog, and the fog leans one way

  • Every prospect has true ratings and a scouting report that shows a range. The board rank you see at the start comes from media buzz, not from the truth.
  • A prospect you have barely seen can be badly misplaced on your board. Each one is misread in a fixed direction, so more scouting moves him as well as narrowing the range.
  • Reports run from Barely seen to Partially scouted, Well scouted and Fully scouted. A Fully scouted prospect shows his true numbers.
  • A report costs 8 scouting points. You start the season with 60, earn about 10 a week, and get 40 more before the draft. Five reports fully work one prospect, so scout a shortlist, not all 60.
  • A better scouting director and a stronger analytics facility make every report sharper. A prospect playing overseas is harder to read, because the same points buy less.

Combine and private workouts

  • The combine is public and free. It measures the body (wingspan, vertical, sprint, agility) and those numbers are facts, not ranges. It tells you what he is, not whether he can play.
  • Some prospects skip the drills. The combine sharpens his current rating more than his ceiling, so do not read a great test as proof of a star.
  • Workouts are private and scarce. They cost no scouting points and open after the combine. You get between 3 and 9 slots, based on your analytics facility and scouting skill.
  • A workout reveals his true ratings and his personality and traits. Personality is hidden on every prospect until you bring him in, so a locker-room risk or a Gym Rat only shows up here.
  • The price is that rivals notice. Each workout can lift his buzz by up to 3 points, which may move him up the board.

The lottery and tanking

  • Only the first round has a lottery. The top four picks are drawn with weighted odds, worst record first, and the three worst records share a 14% chance at number one. The rest of the lottery teams pick in record order, then the playoff teams.
  • Second-round picks go by record, worst first. The lottery does not touch them.
  • The odds shown on screen are the odds the draw uses, so read them before you decide to tank.
  • Tanking is allowed but never free. Sitting healthy stars, selling veterans and playing the bench costs you fan interest and hurts you with free agents. After the deadline the league office sends a memo, and if you keep at it your lottery weight is capped at the sixth slot.
  • An honest bad team on the auto rotation is not punished. The pick still improves with every loss.
  • About one draft class in three has a franchise-changing prospect. A rebuild is worth the most in those years.

Draft night

  • There are two rounds, and the mock draft shows who is likely to be left at your pick. It reads public rankings only, so it cannot see through the fog.
  • Auto-pick uses the same board the AI teams use, weighing ceiling and current rating, blurred by your scouting director. A weak scout makes the same mistakes a weak rival would.
  • When you are on the clock in the first round, a team further down may call to move up. It offers its own later pick tonight plus a future pick, at a premium over the trade chart. About a third of first-round picks get a call, and a declined call does not ring again.
  • Draft night grades you against the board. Getting a player four or more spots later than his rank reads as good value, and ten or more as a steal. Taking one six or more spots earlier reads as a reach.
  • First-round picks get rookie-scale deals of 2 to 4 years. When one ends you can tender a restricted free agent and match offers. Second-round picks get two-year deals with no scale.
  • An overseas pick can be stashed. He takes no roster spot and no cap hold, and you keep his rights for three summers.

How the AI values a trade

  • The trade machine weighs talent, age, upside and contract. Picks are valued by where the team is expected to finish, and far-off picks drift toward the middle of the first round.
  • A protected pick is worth less than it looks, because it often does not convey. A swap right is worth about a third more than the plain pick.
  • Depth is discounted. The best piece in a package counts in full and each extra piece counts less, so a pile of role players cannot buy a superstar.
  • A trade can hold at most five assets per side.
  • Offers in your inbox have already passed the AI's own test, and each is worth at least 85% of what it asks for. They are not gifts and not insults. You can counter once.
  • Calls come about once a week, capped at 14 a season. Contenders buy good players, and rebuilders buy youth. Use the trade finder when you know the player you want gone or the player you want.
  • A player you acquired by trade is locked for 60 game days. An offer the AI sent you is exempt.

Salary rules that block a deal

  • Over the cap, the salary you take back must be within 125% of what you send, plus 0.1 million, or within your cap room plus what you send. The refusal message tells you the exact number.
  • Past the first apron the match is 100%. Past the second apron you cannot combine contracts to take back one bigger contract, and you cannot take back more than you send.
  • Cash can sweeten a close deal. The limit is 5% of the cap each season, it comes out of the owner's budget and it does not touch the cap or the tax. A team over the second apron cannot send cash.
  • The Stepien rule stops you trading away first-round picks in two drafts in a row, and it stops the AI too.
  • Sign-and-trade works only in free agency, for your own free agent whose rights you hold. The partner needs a legal trade and must stay under the apron.
  • Three-team trades need all three teams to say yes, and the salary has to work for each one. The trade deadline is in early February and it is a hard stop.

Questions

Does scouting ever show the true rating?

Yes, but only when a prospect is Fully scouted or you have worked him out. Before that you see a range that leans in one fixed direction, so it can be wrong.

Can I tank on purpose?

You can, but the game charges for it. Fans and free agents react, and the league office can cap your lottery weight at the sixth slot. Your pick still improves with every loss.

Why does the AI refuse a package of role players?

Depth is discounted. The best piece counts in full and the rest count for less, so quantity cannot replace a star.

Can I flip a player I just traded for?

Not right away. A player you acquired by trade is locked for 60 game days, though an offer the AI sent you is exempt.
